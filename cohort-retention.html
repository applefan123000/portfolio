Using your own data

CSV format — one row per cohort per month

Three columns are all you need. Download the template to see it filled in.

  • cohort_month — signup month, YYYY-MM (e.g. 2025-03)
  • months_since_signup — 0 for the signup month, 1 for the month after, and so on
  • active_users — how many of that cohort were active in that month

A wide layout works too: cohort_month, cohort_size, m0, m1, m2, … with active-user counts across the row. Either way, the month-0 count defines the cohort size, and every later month is divided by it.

How to build this from raw events

In SQL or a spreadsheet: take each user's first-seen month as their cohort, then for every later month count distinct users from that cohort who show any activity. One row per cohort × month is the whole input.

Reading the results

  • Read down a column to compare cohorts at the same age — is retention improving over time?
  • Read across a row to see one cohort age — where is the steepest drop?
  • Read the diagonals to spot calendar effects: a bad product month hurts every cohort at once.
  • Young cohorts have short rows. Never compare a 2-month-old cohort's M6 against a mature one — it doesn't exist yet.
Retention = active users in month N ÷ cohort size (month 0). Averages are weighted by cohort size. Everything runs in your browser — uploaded files never leave this page.
